# Monster Master Capture Cube Exterior Form Factor

## Decision

Ordinary capture cubes are **small handheld devices**. A cube's interior living space is much larger than its exterior dimensions and is not constrained by the physical volume of the device.

A trainer may carry ordinary cubes individually, on a belt or harness, or inside compact cases, racks, satchels, lockers, carts, or other storage. A cart can therefore contain many cube cases without any one ordinary capture cube being cage-sized.

## Consequences

- Narration and generated art must not portray an ordinary capture cube as a human-sized cage, crate, kennel, or shipping container merely because the interior accommodates a large monster.
- Opening scenes and scene-object projections should distinguish a **cube**, **cube case/rack**, and **relocation container/equipment** when those distinctions matter.
- The handheld exterior does not make the interior cramped. Existing lore about private living spaces, furniture, food generation, accommodations, monster self-exit, and premium interiors remains unchanged.
- Ancient cubes may have unusual exterior designs, but an unusual design does not automatically imply a physically huge container.
- Purpose-built relocation, quarantine, prison, industrial, or high-capacity equipment may use larger external housings when explicitly authored. Those are specialist devices, not the default capture cube form factor.
- GameFrame asset roles should use handheld cube silhouettes/icons for ordinary devices and separate asset identities for cases, racks, carts, and specialist containment equipment.

## Runtime rule

The CampaignPackage and Dungeon Master should treat ordinary capture-cube exterior scale as a setting invariant. A campaign may introduce an explicitly unusual cube or containment device, but the model may not infer giant exterior size from interior accommodation size.

## Governing rule

> Ordinary capture cubes fit in the hand; the impossible amount of living space is on the inside.
